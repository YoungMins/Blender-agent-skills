## Root causes (hatted, creature)

1. **Pipeline: `surface_nets` kept only the largest connected group of surface cells.** The silhouettes of both cases are legitimately disconnected: the staff and orb stand apart from the robe, the creature's legs and tail segments are separated from the body by small gaps (and the hat floats over the head, see 2). Everything but the largest group was thrown away, so the hull ended at the shoulders (hatted: height 0.30 m of 0.68 m) or lost its legs and tail (creature). Fix: `surface_nets(..., min_island_frac)`; the hull keeps every group with at least 1 % of the largest one's cell count (hull param `island_min_frac`, default 0.01; 1 restores the old behaviour). Phantom specks are still removed by the existing area-based island removal (`remove_islands_ratio`).
2. **Benchmark case: the skin colour of `hatted` was almost the background grey**, so the background flood fill entered the gap between hat brim and shoulders and ate the head out of the reference mask (the scoring GT still has a head). `make_synthetic.py` now uses a darker skin and a new `check_area` guard compares the front reference mask area with the ground-truth alpha silhouette (within 2 %) and refuses to write a case whose mask loses a part. The hatted reference images and cases were regenerated (geometry unchanged).

Separation of the two for `hatted` (hull-only, one run each): island fix on the old, head-less reference: held-out IoU 76.2 %, F@2% 84; island fix plus the corrected reference: 87.3 %, 86. For `creature` the island fix alone gives 90.2 % and F@2% 77 (its references were fine).

Not causes: the thin brim and the staff did not break the run pairing (`pair_max_aspect`) and calibration was not anchored on them, the orientation / camera_axis of the cases is right, and a tail beyond the front silhouette is not a problem.

## Limits that remain

- A hull-only reconstruction of a disconnected part has the depth of its silhouette pair, not its true shape: the staff comes out as an ellipse about as deep as the hat above it (F@2% 86, min held-out IoU 78 % at some azimuths). Build thin parts as primitives (tube / disc / cone, see `references/reconstruction-workflow.md`) when that matters.
- `sd_warrior` flags (`occ:arm_*`, `mis:*`) are the part-attribution warnings of the current spec, not failures.

## Root cause

Not the run pairing: the fox's carve masks have one run per row for most slices (the many-run slices are the hair tufts, below). The cause is the per-slice cross-section itself. `_cross_section_term` inscribes a superellipse in the front x-run x side y-run of **every Z slice**, so the run extent of one pixel row sets the width of the *whole ring* of that slice, not just of the outline. Hair spikes (a tuft 8-20 px tall sticking out 5-8 % of the half-width) and the stepped hair tiers of the fox make the run extent jump by 5-20 % from slice to slice; every jump becomes a ledge that runs around the entire circumference (a lampshade). The silhouette itself is right (front/side error 3.9 / 6.9 %); the references cannot see the other azimuths, and the superellipse prior copies the outline ripple into all of them.

Evidence (fox body, run-4 masks, resolution 320, `slice_coherence` 0): the half-width of the front mask at the head rows goes 10.3 -> 11.4 -> 10.8 -> 10.3 -> 11.3 -> 10.6 cm within 6 cm of height, and the front/side run counts per slice are `(1,1)` in only 123 of ~330 slices (up to 12 x-runs in the tuft rows); the same hull with `cross_section: rectangle` (pure silhouette prism) has no ring at the diagonal azimuths but is square. The radial ring metric below on the head band (z 0.42-0.50 m) is 2.8 mm RMS before and 0.5 mm after. Smoothing the mesh afterwards cannot work: the ledges are 3-8 voxels tall and 1 cm deep, `smooth_iterations` 40/80 and `post_smooth_iterations` 12/20 only round them (the agent's runs).

## Fix: `slice_coherence` (hull param, default 0.03 of the height; 0 = old behaviour)

`core/carve.py`: the run extents that size the superellipses are grey-opened along Z with an element of `slice_coherence x height` (`_open_along_z` / `_regularize_extents`): a bulge thinner than that is flattened to its neighbours, unless it sticks out by more than 30 % of the run half-width (a hat brim, an arm, a horn is a part, not a spike). The raw superellipse survives only as a thin fin around the two carve planes (penalty `0.25 x min(a, b) x |sin 2 phi|`), so the hull still reaches every spike tip and the outline of both reference views: the silhouette is not changed (train error +0.0), only the other azimuths are regularised. No smoothing (a Gaussian shrinks convex shapes: it cost 0.4-1.0 pt silhouette error / 1 pt F@2% on chibi and hatted in a first attempt).

## Ring metric (`core/ringmetric.py`, `bench/score.py`)

Per Z slice the outer radius from the slice centroid in 48 azimuth sectors, excluding the sectors within +-26 deg of the four carve axes (those are supported by a silhouette); high-pass along Z (Gaussian, sigma = 1.5 % of the height); median over the sectors (a ring has the same sign all around, a local fin does not); RMS over the body slices (mean radius >= 40 % of the maximum, filled area >= 70 % of the disc, |residual| < 10 % of the radius so a brim is a part, not a ring); divided by the mean radius, in %. `ring:<id>` is flagged above 2.4 % (`ringmetric.FLAG_PCT`). Measured on the carved field inside the pipeline (`hull.py` writes `report.hull.<id>.rings`), so it works for cases without ground truth. A smooth sphere scores 0.3, the synthetic spiky sphere of `tests/test_slice_coherence.py` 3.1 -> 0.9 (`slice_coherence` 0.03). The whole-body RMS is diluted by legit features (boots, torso): the fox's hair band drops 5x but the total only 2.8 -> 2.2, hence the soft threshold; use `ring_score` on a z-range for local checks.

## Limits

* The fox head is still a drum with faint horizontal creases and a flat top (the ears are excluded parts), plus thin spike fins at the sides; the texture projection streaks on the flat top. The rings (ledged tiers) are gone, the hair does not become a 3D hairstyle: build locks as `cone` / `blade` parts if the 3D look matters.
* Features that are both thinner than `slice_coherence` x height in Z and smaller than 30 % of the run width (a fine belt, a collar ridge) lose their ring around the diagonal azimuths (they stay at the carve planes). `slice_coherence: 0` restores the old hull; 0.02 keeps more detail, 0.04-0.05 flattens bigger tiers.

# hatted regression: body-axis origin vs the ground-truth frame (2026-10-23)

`runs/final` (after the body-axis origin change) showed hatted held-out IoU 87.3 -> 70.7, F@2% 86 -> 61, Chamfer 0.97 -> 1.87 (also `__noacc`, `__scale2`); train error stayed 2.8 %. Other cases unchanged.

**Root cause (bisected: `BODY_AXIS_MIN_SHIFT` = 10, i.e. body axis off, restores 87.3 / 86 / 0.97).** The staff drags the front / back bbox centre 2.8 cm off the body axis, so the model origin moves to the body axis. The recon is the same shape translated by +24.6 mm in X (recon bbox centre x = +0.0246, ground truth 0), front and back moved together and the side view (Y) is unaffected, so the model is internally consistent; the benchmark's ground truth and held-out camera are centred on the bbox, so every held-out render and every mesh distance was shifted. Not causes: `slice_coherence`, `remove_enclosed`, inpaint / normals, `lathe` (the toggled run keeps them all).

**Fix (trade-off).** The body-axis origin stays (it is what makes `mirror_of`, the mirrored side colour and the symmetry metric work about the true centre line: penguin symmetry 0.965). The pipeline now reports `frame_offset_m` = {x, y} (bbox centre minus origin, `core/calibration.py::frame_offset_m`, in `report.json`); `bench/score.py` subtracts it from the recon mesh and `bench/render_holdout.py` moves the held-out camera target by it. Cost: a ground-truth frame that is not bbox-centred needs that offset (real inputs have none). Guard: `tests/test_calibration_frame.py` projects the ground-truth mesh through the calibrated front / back / side views of `hatted` and asserts that front and back agree in world X, all baselines and the side origin agree within 2 mm, and that the reported offset is the front / back shift.

`runs/final2` (full bench, `--jobs 2`, final code of that day) vs `runs/final`: hatted 70.7 -> 87.5 IoU, 61 -> 86 F@2%, 1.87 -> 1.0 Chamfer; hatted__noacc 70.5 -> 86.7, hatted__scale2 71.0 -> 87.6; no other case moves (max |dIoU| 0.0, |dF@2%| 0.0, |dtrain| 0.0). New regression case `sd_twintail` (blind run 6, strand tails; no ground truth).

`runs/final3` (full bench, `--jobs 2`) vs `runs/final2`: `partfit` now fits `strand` parts (points + radius per point + flatten, p0 anchored, second-difference smoothness, soft `tail_l`/`tail_r` symmetry, <= 60 s per strand). Only `sd_twintail` moves: total error 11.6 -> 7.8 % (front 12.0 -> 7.7, back 13.2 -> 9.0, side 9.6 -> 6.8; unfitted strands were 11.6, the blind fitted cones 10.4), failed_validation -> passed (threshold 8.0; excess over the 8.0 % front/back floor: front -0.4, back +1.0); tail label IoU 68 / 69 -> 76 / 80 %.
